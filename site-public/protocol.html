C-017 Verify Spec

Cryptographic integrity is not enough for AI decisions.

A signature can prove that a decision record remained unchanged. It cannot prove that the evidence justified the decision.

Most work in this area stops at integrity: hash the record, sign it, chain it, timestamp it. That is useful, and it is now widely available. It answers one question — is this the same record that was committed? It leaves a second question untouched: did the evidence support what was concluded, and should a conclusion reached yesterday constrain what a system is allowed to decide tomorrow?

VEDM — Verifiable Evidentiary Decision & Memory — is an attempt at that second layer. It treats a seal as a commitment inside an evidentiary chain rather than the end of the process.

evidence  →  predicate  →  resolution  →  binding constraint  →  decision  →  commitment

An ablation, not a diagram

The distinction is only worth stating if it can be tested. The same decision was run twice. Everything was held fixed except whether a previously committed resolution was binding.

The decision moved. The model was not instructed to weigh the earlier resolution more heavily. A deterministic layer outside the model evaluated the binding constraint and eliminated the non-compliant candidate before the model's output was accepted.

How the candidate was eliminated

This does not prove that provider_b is the correct decision.

It does not establish that binding historical resolutions improve decision quality in general.

It shows something narrower: a committed resolution can constrain the set of decisions available later — not by being retrieved as context, but by removing options before an answer is accepted.

The integrity layer underneath

VEDM addresses evidentiary semantics and binding decision state. ISCProof is the open format carrying the integrity guarantee beneath it: a portable evidence pack binding a content hash, a timestamp and a signature into one verifiable unit.
